# Retrospective Kalshi forecasting protocol

Frozen locally on 28 September 2026, before cohort collection and held-out scoring. The earlier one-contract API schema probe informed this design. This document is not an external preregistration, and outcomes already exist in the public API. Any amendments must be dated and explained; no changes based on held-out performance are permitted.

## Population and split

Collect binary BTC and ETH 15-minute contracts (`KXBTC15M`, `KXETH15M`) with scheduled close times at an exact UTC hour in [3 August 2026 00:00 UTC, 28 September 2026 00:00 UTC). This deterministic hourly subsample limits collection and overlap without selecting by trading volume, outcome, or forecast error. The nominal maximum is 2,688 contracts. Keep both series for every eligible hour.

Training: 3–30 August (28 UTC dates). Validation: 31 August–13 September (14 dates). Test: 14–27 September (14 dates). Assign by close date, keeping both assets and all intraday contracts together. No random contract split. The sample size is a bounded feasibility study, not a power-based confirmatory design.

Require a unique ticker/event mapping, a 900-second open-to-close window, binary yes/no resolution, and nonprovisional final settlement. Preserve all raw candidate metadata and an exclusion ledger. Audit timestamp rules in the contract text. Retrieve true `volume_fp` from one-minute candles; retain bid/ask and transaction OHLC separately. Do not derive volume from prices or substitute open interest. Check candle sums against lifetime volume when complete full-window candles are available, and audit selected intervals against fully paginated trades if feasible.

## Forecast information and feature construction

Primary forecast: five minutes before scheduled close. Secondary forecast: one minute before close. Apply a 60-second data-availability buffer at both horizons: use only candles with `end_period_ts <= close_ts - lead_seconds - 60`. Thus the final settlement averaging minute contributes no features. This is a retrospective reconstruction, not a recorded live forecast; the buffer is assumed rather than verified exchange publication latency.

Use every complete one-minute candle from open+60 through the data cutoff. Require finite transaction-close prices in [0,1], nonnegative finite interval volumes, unique regular timestamps, and a positive-volume trade in every retained candle. Exclude missing/no-trade prefixes from the common comparison cohort at that horizon and count reasons. No forward fills. No future candle, lifetime volume, settlement price, final quote, or outcome enters features. Market metadata supplies the scheduled time and label only.

Clip prices to [0.001,0.999] for logit features; raw-price Brier scores remain unclipped, and all reported log scores use this common clipping rule. Repeat scores and feature fitting with [0.005,0.995] as a declared boundary sensitivity. Use the first retained close as the initial-price benchmark; model increments begin after it. The first candle's volume may enter supervised history summaries; conditional increment likelihoods use only subsequent candles.

Fit scalers on training contracts separately by horizon. Use `log1p(volume)` for supervised predictors and divide actual interval volume by the training median positive volume for the mixture. Do not fit scalers to validation/test data. All methods use the same eligible contracts per horizon.

## Fixed model family

Report: training-frequency prior; initial transaction price; latest available transaction price; logistic recalibration of the latest price; ridge logistic history predictor; conditional finite-mixture likelihood; and its no-volume ablation. The history predictor uses initial and latest logits, logit change over the final three increments, sum of squared increments, mean log-volume, its linear time slope, and a volume-weighted increment sum. Add an asset indicator to fitted logistic predictors. These are descriptive features, not inferred trader intent.

Fit the conditional mixture using training outcomes and price increments only. Use a finite nuisance catalogue with a shared state over each path, two directional Gaussian components and a centered Student-t component. Normalize densities exactly, integrate each full-path likelihood over the catalogue, and fit catalogue weights by training likelihood. Scales and catalogue construction use training data only. Record exact constants in a model-design addendum before accessing test scores. The empirical mixture is an adaptation of the paper's model, not the synthetic generating law.

The raw likelihood forecast combines a training-frequency outcome prior with its path log Bayes factor. This treats observed volumes as a conditional design and remains a potentially misspecified classifier; it does not establish exogenous volumes. Also report a ridge logistic combination of latest-price logit, path log Bayes factor, and asset indicator; name it explicitly as a calibrated combination, not a coherent second update of the market price. Fit that combination on validation only to avoid training-density reuse. Choose logistic ridge penalty from {0.01,0.1,1,10} by validation log loss for training-fitted baselines; fit the combination with fixed penalty 1. No test tuning. Include the no-volume version using the same training design and normalized volumes fixed to one.

## Evaluation and uncertainty

Freeze prediction files and fitting artifacts before computing test scores. Primary metric is Brier loss, with log loss, calibration-bin counts, and accuracy as secondary descriptions. Main contrasts: mixture versus recalibrated latest price; calibrated mixture/price combination versus recalibrated price; history logistic versus recalibrated price. Report volume ablation and both horizons without selecting a favorable result. Separate assets descriptively.

Report paired mean loss differences and 95% percentile intervals from 5,000 bootstrap resamples of UTC dates (both assets and every sampled contract on each date stay together), seed 20260929. Fourteen test dates give limited precision, and this bootstrap does not protect against dependence across dates. Include date-level differences and their signs so one day cannot silently drive the conclusion. Equal weight per contract is primary; report counts by date and asset. Calibration uses fixed bins [0,.1,...,1]. No profit claims; transaction closes are forecasts, not guaranteed executable quotes.

## Limits and decision rules

Proceed regardless of direction or significance. If the mixture fails, describe that failure and its assumptions. A positive comparison is restricted to this sampled short-horizon crypto cohort. No broad market claim without a separate domain and later replication. Do not move dates, change features, or enlarge the sample in response to test scores. Data/API failures may require documented feasibility amendments before scoring; preserve original rules.

## Design clarification before prediction or scoring

28 September 2026: add a validation-fitted price-only logistic control, with asset indicator and fixed ridge penalty 1, to match the calibration window and penalty of the mixture/price combinations. Their main incremental-information contrast uses this matched control. Keep the training-fitted, validation-selected price recalibrator and all originally planned comparisons. This avoids attributing an advantage from a newer calibration period to the path likelihood. No held-out score has been computed.

The history feature called a volume-weighted increment sum is implemented as `sum(v_t*z_t)/sum(v_t)` over increments after the initial close, so it is invariant to volume units. Log-volume trend is the ordinary least-squares slope against equally spaced, centered minute indices. Logistic fitting minimizes summed Bernoulli negative log likelihood plus half the stated penalty times the squared standardized slopes; its intercept is unpenalized. The mixture uses a Jeffreys-smoothed training outcome prior, whereas the simple prior benchmark uses the empirical training frequency. The complete mixture formula and fitted-state procedure are fixed in `empirical_model_design.md`.

28 September 2026, still before predictions or test scoring: include a dependence-aware Gaussian history comparator tied to the theoretical extension. For each asset, estimate separate outcome-class mean increment vectors and a shared residual covariance from training paths. Fix covariance regularization to 90% sample covariance plus 10% its diagonal, with diagonal variance floor 0.0001. Use a Jeffreys-smoothed asset-specific training outcome prior. This is a joint Gaussian density over the full increment vector, without volume covariates. Report its raw probabilities and a validation-fitted combination of its log Bayes factor with latest-price logit and asset indicator (fixed ridge penalty 1), using the matched recent price calibrator as control. No tuning of covariance regularization. These are prescoring additions, recorded because the earlier protocol asked for a dependence-aware comparator.

Final independent review, before prediction or scoring: strengthen that recent endpoint control to an asset-specific cubic logistic calibration. Its seven predictors are latest logit x, x², x³, ETH indicator e, e*x, e*x², and e*x³. Every validation-fitted mixture or Gaussian combination uses these identical seven predictors plus its log Bayes factor. Fix penalty 1 for all. This supersedes the earlier linear specification for recent calibration/combinations; the original training-fitted linear price comparator remains unchanged. The added control reduces the risk of mistaking nonlinear or asset-specific endpoint recalibration for extra history information. A fitted cubic still does not equal the theoretically optimal endpoint predictor.

Scalers for training-fitted models use training only. The explicitly validation-fitted calibration stage fits its own standardization on validation, identically for its price control and combinations; prediction never refits scalers. Audit settlement availability at the split boundaries: every training label must settle before the first validation forecast, and every validation label before the first test forecast. Abort if either fails. Metadata availability checks use timestamps without reading forecast errors.

Numerical implementation note, before any completed prediction lock or test score: a training logistic fit terminated its L-BFGS line search at a maximum gradient of 1.72e-6 for summed loss (1.30e-9 per observation). The pipeline now accepts such a termination only when the gradient per observation is at most 1e-8, recording the original optimizer status and gradient. The objective, penalty choices, data splits, and model family are unchanged.
